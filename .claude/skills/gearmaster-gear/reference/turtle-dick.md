# Naming gear in the *Tales from the Crypt* theme

Everything here was derived by re-casting all 374 catalogue entries at once.
The rules are what made that batch come out consistent; they are what will keep
piece 375 consistent with it.

## The world in one paragraph

Francis the Gambler is a ~900-year-old immortal in a coat sewn from $100 bills
(actually fur from a Wimpler Ox, p. 93), cursed to live until he wins a
one-in-a-billion lottery. He planeswalks; when a plane annoys him he flattens it
to a tapestry. Magic is **the Funny** (pp. 46-50): comedic energy channelled
through goofs — a banana peel immolates soldiers, a pie detonates a mecha-suit,
enough self-deprecation drops a moon. Low-level goofs "often go horribly wrong,"
which is where the game's failure branches come from. The player is a
**Sprocketman**: gear-folk mined out of the Great Gear Cave by Lord Drabley
Henpeck over the Deep Chocolate beneath it (p. 44).

## Substance ladders — the load-bearing rule

A player ranks a shelf by reading the first word. That has to keep working after
every word changes, so the theme spends its substances as **grades**:

**Defence** — Cork → Vinyl → Sneel → Time-Tempered → **Ypytryktrium**

Cork is cheap and everywhere; stainless sneel is Henpeck's good steel (p. 44);
Ypytryktrium is "the heaviest element known to mankind" (p. 28) and is where
Adamant and Godsteel both land.

**Speed** — Slow Trundler → Fast Roller → **Thrumbus**

From the 45th Annual Thrumbus Race (CSV #11). Sevenleague and Swift-anything go
to Thrumbus; Plain goes to Slow Trundler.

**Sacred** — Francian → **Wimpler**

The Francians are the calculator-fanatics who made Francis their god by accident
(pp. 61-63); Consecrated, Sanctified and Votive are theirs. Wimpler Oxen are the
sacred floating space-bison (pp. 91-93) — Aether and Godsheet are theirs.

**Cold** is a *place*, not a rung: **Dobira**, the mountain (pp. 46-50). Glacier,
Hoarfrost, Rime and Pilgrim all point at it. Frostbind is the exception —
**Nut Freeze**, from Nut Metropolis.

A new bottom-tier plating is a Cork something. A new top-tier one is
Ypytryktrium. **Do not invent a sixth metal** — one more substance means the
player can no longer rank a shelf without opening six tooltips, and that is the
entire thing these ladders buy.

## Every name comes from the book

Not "sounds vaguely sci-fi" — actually from the text, and you should be able to
give the page. A name you invented is a name that will contradict something on
p. 90 later. Sources, roughly in order of how often they paid off:

- **Substances**: cork, sneel, Ypytryktrium, octarine dust, Time Sap, ench,
  Fnorp, Plug Energy, silicon radishes, Chromatic Rice, Deep Chocolate
- **Places**: Bambulon, Treyway, Kaplin, Multicity, Petonkle, Dobira, Kolok,
  the Mansus, Fragment City, Burger Eden, the Soda Labyrinth, Nut Metropolis
- **Creatures**: Wimpler Oxen, Thrumbuses, Hell Pigeons, Grosk Wurms, Titan
  Megalodons, Sabre-toothed Frongs, the Brumpus, goosters, Least Weasels,
  wallspiders, LETO the Worm
- **Cast**: Skink Brink, Galapagos Jim, Gappy Handman, Spike Kaklon, Nibbalonius,
  Ponkey Dong, the Master of Dobira, Marbulon, Cashew Kid, FrogDog
- **CSV titles**: the unwritten three-quarters are free real estate. *Spooky
  Action a Mile Away* became Arc Lightning; *Comedy Bomb*, *Copy Paste Race*,
  *The Floor is Guava*, *Time Harp Waltz* all landed somewhere.

When genuinely nothing fits, write a plain descriptive name in the book's
register — "Betting Stick," "Banana-Peel Grip" — rather than fabricating a
proper noun that implies lore the book does not have.

## The content charter

The build is public, so the theme uses the book's clean half - with one
deliberate exception, added after the fact.

**Crude character names are in.** The owner asked for them by name: Big Yomp,
and PoopFart, which is what the book actually calls the Weeping Seeker. Bodily
-function humour is allowed *in names*. This reverses the original rule, which
had the Seeker referred to only by his epithet.

**Still excluded outright:** sexual and anatomical humour; drugs, alcohol and
smoking (semuta stays as *music*; Corquewine, B-quila, shermsticks and
Skunkweed do not appear; Blingarian Corona Rum is only ever a nameless "searing
liqueur," and never in game text); slur-adjacent coinages (the LSB calves on
p. 31, "Las Gayass" on p. 42); every real public figure.

**Renamed where the concept is good and the name is not** - the *Autoloading Net
Ejaculator* (p. 28) is the **Net Launcher**; "FitAss" is the **Fit Watch**.
Rename the concept and keep it; the book's ideas are better than its labels.

**Violence stays cartoon-grade.** This is a combat game, but flavour text sits at
Saturday-morning level: the Crimper crunches, and nobody is described being
crunched.

**The title gag** is handled the way the book's best story handles it (pp. 19-27):
a boy and his turtle. Nothing else in the game needs to touch it.

## Nine names stay in English

`the_turtle_theme_covers_the_catalogue` fails on any canonical name that maps to
itself unless it is on this list, and the list carries its reasons:

| kept | why |
|---|---|
| Ratchet Cog, Flywheel Cog | cogs are the player's own culture now, salvaged from the Great Gear Cave — the one place the old words survive on purpose |
| Anvil Frame, Hollow Weave | already the book's: the Comedian's anvil; the Mansus walls that are not there |
| Witch's Hat | Marbulon was an old withered witch |
| Green Crown, Wandering Root | already read as Nut Metropolis |
| Worldeye Orb | the Mansus sun-being's gaze |
| The Money Jacket | it *is* Francis's coat |

Adding to it means editing that test. Do it deliberately — an entry there is a
promise that the plain word is the *better* word, not that you ran out of ideas.

## Register

Read a page before you write a name. The book's voice is **deadpan escalation**:
mundane errands colliding with cosmic stakes. Mailing a burger up a mountain.
A 1.79-trillion-person city with lethal commutes and 29-hour bars. The names
that work sound like an errand — *Betting Stick*, *Shelf-Drink Fluid*,
*Trillion-Pound Plate* — and the joke is what they turn out to do. Names that
strain for epic ("Doomreaver of the Void") read as a different book.

Two anchors worth matching:

- **Jokes** are the resource you spend; **Funny** is the harm they do. Keeping
  the two words distinct is what makes "spend 3 jokes to deal 12 funny damage" a
  sentence instead of a tautology. A name should never blur them.
- **Fnorp** is money (p. 28, p. 111). Not gold, not coins.

## Length

The name has to fit a shop card and a cooldown bar. Four words is the practical
ceiling for a component; "Wheel of the Thrumbus Race" is at the limit and only
gets away with it because it is a ring. Prefer two or three.

The procedural generator has a separate rule — word count scales with rarity,
3/4/5/6 for common/rare/epic/legendary, with the short name pinned at two words
for the cooldown bars. That corpus is `TD_NAMING` in `theme.rs`, and it is not
the same job as naming a component: components are hand-named, and the generator
names the *assembled item*.

## The method that made 374 names come out right

Do it as **data first, Rust last**. In order:

1. Write the mapping in a scratch file as `canonical -> themed` pairs.
2. Check every canonical key against `CATALOG` **before** writing any Rust. Four
   names in the original proposal did not exist — `Ironbark`, `Thornmail`,
   `Warding Plating`, `Ash Handle` — and are really `Ironbark Layer`,
   `Thornmail Layer`, `Warding Plate`, and nothing at all. A bad key is not a
   compile error; it is a piece that quietly keeps its plain name.
3. Check the new names against each other for duplicates.
4. Only then generate the table entries and paste them in, sorted.

For a single piece this collapses to: grep the canonical name in `piece.rs` to
confirm it exists, grep the themed name in `theme.rs` to confirm it does not,
then add the line.
