The Two Trades

A quartermaster who buys and packs, a pathfinder who walks and answers, and both of them learned. The second one is told where to go — which is what turns an agent that plays well into a solver that can prove a door is reachable.

Part OneWhy two, and why this split

The predecessor's pilot is 195,000 presses a run and 77% of them are trial-seat-and-undo. No temporal-difference method assigns credit across that.

Splitting the problem is not a preference — it is what makes the horizon learnable at all. The split is by screen, which is how a person meets the game.

The case, in one example

A tray of nature producers and a spell that spends nature for damage scores zero on both halves: the producers have no field, and the spell's damage is conditional, so it is not in stats.physical_damage. The current packer reaches 48/50 by maximising items × 400 — density — which rl-research.md already calls "nearly irrelevant". The one board it cannot recover is the perfect run's: 50/50 by hand, 46–48 by search, and the one most likely to be built around a synergy.

A learner rewarded by the fight sees all of it, because the chain shows up as damage in the log. That is the whole argument.

Part ThreeTen milestones, a commit apiece

On the-two-trades, branched off the-apprentice. One merge, at Q9 — everything before it is tooling that ships nothing.

  1. The ground, and the two menus

    Partition Verb between the two agents, exhaustively and disjointly. Measure the real decision counts at each level.

    Gate: the partition lint passes — every verb belongs to exactly one agent, nothing lost, nothing shared.

  2. The representation

    Feature encoders where a piece is described by what it produces and what it consumes, and a board by the match between them. Not learning — the milestone that decides whether learning can work.

    Gate: a linear probe over the features predicts "this board's damage came from a conditional" better than chance. If it cannot, nothing after this matters.

  3. Two environments

    Reset, step, reward, legal — for both. The quartermaster's reset samples a situation across the whole ladder, using skip_to, which is training-only and unreachable from any agent.

    Gate: horizons inside 60 and 600; both replay identically from a seed.

  4. The quartermaster learns

    DQN over the factored action, masked to the menu. Trained against sampled situations so it is not a rung-10 specialist.

    Gate: ≥ 48/50 from the owner's tray and 49/50 from the friend's — the hand-written packer's own numbers.

  5. Pools, proven

    A planted tray of producers and one consumer: does the learned packer assemble it where the heuristic does not? Plus a census of pool-matching across a hundred boards from each.

    Gate: a measured difference, either way. "The learned packer does not find builds either" is a publishable result and would redirect the mission.

  6. The pathfinder learns

    Goal-conditioned DQN over the road, calling the frozen Q3 checkpoint. Curriculum from near goals to far ones.

    Gate: SCR(Francis) ≥ 40.6%, or a written finding.

  7. The validity solver

    The product. Every door tried as an explicit goal rather than met by accident, and every gap classified in the five classes.

    Gate: coverage above 70% offered and 58% branched, and all seven dungeons entered — the specific failure this was asked to fix.

  8. Generations

    Unfreeze. Retrain the quartermaster on the trays the pathfinder actually produces, then the pathfinder on the new quartermaster.

    Gate: each generation ≥ the last on the held-out 64 seeds, or the mission stops at the best and says so.

  9. Themes

    Train on eight themes, hold two out. What makes the quartermaster usable as the enemy packer.

    Gate: held-out themes beat unconditioned, measured by the fidelity meter under the acceptance gate.

  10. The harvest, and the record

    Creature boards from trained play — the five county creatures that still wear borrowed ones.

    Merge into main, and the wasm publish with it. The only point where the game changes, and the only one needing a diff the owner has read.

Part FourFour things worth arguing about first

Q1 is not a machine-learning milestone, and it is the one that decides

If the state cannot express "this piece makes nature and that one spends it", no amount of Q-learning discovers the build — it learns the current blindness faster. The gate is a probe, and a failed probe stops everything.

"In parallel" has to mean parallel with generations

Their rewards share no term, which is what makes simultaneous training reasonable. But the pathfinder's environment is the quartermaster, so it always trains against a frozen, versioned checkpoint, swapped at generation boundaries. Every checkpoint names the version it trained against; a result that cannot name both does not replay. It costs a little joint optimality and buys the only thing that matters early: when a number moves, you know which agent moved it.

The reward asymmetry is enforced by the crate graph, not by discipline

The quartermaster's reward is a fight, which a player cannot run — so the trainer holds the oracle and the agent reads only the screen. That is the asymmetric actor–critic, and trades/tests/boundary.rs makes it checkable exactly as the pilot's does.

Recorded rather than argued

The environment is deterministic and cheap, so search with a learned value will probably beat model-free Q in the end. Q is the simpler thing that works and the right first implementation, and no work is wasted: if generations plateau, the escalation is MCTS over the pathfinder's actions using the same value head.

Two stop conditions worth agreeing now

Q4 finding no difference retires this mission's premise, and the honest response is to write it down and keep the hand-written packer.

Q5 landing below 40.6% is likely early and is not failure. The goal-conditioning is the product: a goal-seeker at 30% that reaches every door is worth more than a climber at 41% that has entered one dungeon in seven.
