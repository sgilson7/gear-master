Part OneThe codebase

Three crates, one rule: the engine holds every rule and no graphics.

crates/engine is the game. crates/cli is a headless REPL that drives it, and crates/gui is a macroquad window that drives the same engine — 10,832 lines of it, the largest single file in the workspace. A scripted run piped into the CLI replays identically; that is the design contract, not a convenience.

The engine's weight sits in two files. piece.rs (9,020 lines) defines what a piece is and then lists all 446 of them. combat.rs (5,111 lines) runs the fight and carries all 57 monster boards inside it. Everything else is small and single-purpose: rating, naming, classes, shop, towns, events, rumours, dungeons, share codes, and a display-only theme layer.

The four doctrines

  1. Determinism is load-bearing. Combat consults no RNG at all. One seeded PRNG exists for shop stock and other out-of-combat draws. Even the Ticket to Ride miss chance is deterministic.
  2. Canonical names are string keys. Piece names key the theme table, monster gear lists, quest targets, event and rumour conditions, and dozens of tests. Renaming without propagating is the repo's classic failure.
  3. Tests pin behavior on purpose. Rarity distribution and fight outcomes are pinned so a batch of new content cannot quietly move them. A moved pin gets re-pinned with a written justification, never loosened.
  4. A theme cannot break the game. theme.rs is display-only; missing entries fall through to canonical names.
Verified

A clean checkout builds and runs green: 29 test binaries, 492 tests, zero failures. That is the baseline every re-pin below will be measured against.

Part TwoThe game

You pack five grids. The engine turns what you packed into a fight that could not have gone any other way.

The road

Fifty creature rungs carry three bosses and seven mini-bosses. Three towns sit between rungs, events stand in front of them, and dungeons stand beside them — a run that enters all three towns stands on 53 rungs, and nothing on the road can be walked past. Fountains appear before each boss, score the build on axes, and hand out classes; the third can double one. Milestone rungs re-price the shop, named creatures leave their gear behind, and a kill under two seconds in the first ten rungs opens the casino exactly once.

A fight

Both boards tick in 50ms steps. Items activate on cooldown — their own, or their slot's default. A hit is (flat damage + strength) × power, typed physical or magic; the defender answers with the matching resist, punched through by pierce and shored up by harden. Armor absorbs first and resets to zero every fight. Regen heals per second, Grow raises max health mid-fight, and mind damage lowers max health and cannot be healed. Curses stack by kind with caps and floors. Stalemates run the full clock.

Pools and assembly

Mana is fuel. The other three pools pay passively at exact rates: rage gives +1 physical damage a point, faith +2 physical and +2 magic resist, nature +1 regen. Loose pieces contribute their flat stats; pieces connected into a recipe become an item that acts. Rating scores a board, rating sets price and rarity, and rarity sets how many words the generated name gets — three for Common up to six for Legendary. Which is why the rarity thresholds are untouchable: move them and every item in the game is renamed.

The problem the rewrite exists to fix

The weapon is the only slot that asks a question. It owns three recipes, all the damage, most of the curse game, and most of the reaction game. The other four are one stat-pile slot wearing four shapes — helmet and chest sit at 0.93 cosine similarity, and a single k-means cluster absorbs 353 of the pieces across every slot. The fix is redistribution, not nerfs: give each slot one basis vector and move the weapon's side-monopolies out to the slots that should have owned them.

Scope, restated honestly

197 pieces — 44% of the catalog — are pure stat lines. Every armor slot is over half inert. The spec's quota caps plain filler at 15% per slot, which means roughly 130–150 pieces need mechanical content written for them from scratch. That is not a sweep of off-axis offenders; it is authoring a third of a game's worth of new gear behavior. This is the single largest cost in the rewrite and the spec does not name it.

The reaction slot has no reactions

Gloves — the designated exclusive home of the reaction game — currently carry zero OnAdjacentActivate triggers. All nine live on weapon (6), helmet (2) and chest (1). The migration is not "move most of them"; it is "build the slot's identity from nothing."

Part FourWhere the spec is wrong

The repo's rule is that the design document wins and the code files a bug report. That rule assumes the document describes intent. Where the document makes a factual claim about the code and the claim is false, the document is what needs amending — and it does, in one structural place.

The bleed cycle contradicts the recipes

The spec's organizing device is a directed 5-cycle: Weapon → Gloves → Greaves → Chest → Helmet → Weapon, so every slot overlaps exactly one neighbor. §3.5 grounds the Greaves→Chest link in shipped code — "the recipe's optional Plating is the armor bleed, already shipped."

It isn't. PieceDef::fits lets two piece kinds cross grids, and they don't cross where the spec says:

Cross-grid pieces — what fits() actually permits
KindCountDeclared homeAlso placeable in
Material37Gloves 20 · Greaves 17Gloves ↔ Greaves
Plating24Helmet 23 · Greaves 1Helmet ↔ Greaves

Plating is a helmet kind that floats to greaves. Chest takes Base + Layer only and cannot receive a Plating at all. So the structural adjacency the code ships is not a 5-cycle — it is a path, Gloves — Greaves — Helmet, with Weapon and Chest isolated at the ends. Greaves is already the crossroads slot.

Consequence for the exclusivity table

61 pieces (13.7%) can be legally placed outside their declared slot. A test can only enforce exclusivity on def.slot; at runtime a greaves Material carrying OnBattleStart sits happily in the gloves grid. "Greaves-exclusive" is therefore a statement about authorship, not about the board — unless the identity-carrying mechanics are deliberately kept off the two floating kinds.

That last clause is also the repair. If Material and Plating are treated as the designated bleed carriers — deliberately neutral, deliberately shared — and the identity mechanics live on the kinds that cannot float (Mold, Ring, Crest, Frame, Base, Layer), then the exclusivity table becomes enforceable and the bleed structure stops being invented: it is read off the recipes the game already ships.

Part FiveThe plan

Nine changes, in dependency order, each one shippable and each one green. The gates are the point: no catalog work begins until the test that judges it exists and fails.

  1. Baseline metrics harness

    A census binary over CATALOG and a deterministic CLI replay that reports per-slot damage share and per-rung time-to-kill for three reference builds (melee, caster, hybrid) at rungs 10, 25 and 40. Read-only: attribution comes from pairing Hit events to the preceding Activate index, so no engine change ships here.

    Gate · numbers committed to analysis/. They are the denominators for every later claim.

  2. catalog_shape.rs, written red

    The exclusivity table and both quota families — axis quotas and interaction-density quotas — encoded as ranges, not exact counts. It fails loudly against today's catalog and stays failing until PR 6. Enforced on def.slot; Material and Plating are exempted as designated bleed carriers, and the plain-filler assertion is set at 30% with the 15% target recorded beside it.

    Gate · red for the right reasons — each assertion names the piece that broke it.

  3. Engine primitives, part one

    Watch counters, the diagonal relation, and fusion pools. Each deterministic, integer-only, and unit-tested before any piece uses them. Fusion grows Resource to seven; the compiler finds the match sites and Resource::index() gets the careful read.

    Gate · identical logs across two replays of one seed. No catalog changes in this PR.

  4. Underlay placement

    Overlap as terrain, merged alone because it touches placement, packing, rating, the auto-builder and the GUI at once. Legality checks read the board's current rows, never SLOT_H. If it balloons, the documented fallback is adjacency-shaped approximation — but it is the fallback, not the plan.

    Gate · packing.rs and taller_boards.rs unmoved.

  5. Weapon exodus, greaves intake

    Curse concepts leave the weapon for new greaves pieces; reaction triggers leave for gloves designs; Drain and StunStrongest go to the hands. Searing stays weapon-majority — it is damage wearing a curse costume. Greaves gain the 10–15 new pieces they need, appended to CATALOG, never inserted.

    Gate · no damage number on any weapon piece changed. The weapon loses scope, not power.

  6. Helmet and chest sweep

    Helmet takes Consume, empowerment, shield, mind damage and mind_resist and gives up its armor piles; chest takes the Grow and harden monopolies and grows its reserve numbers to absorb what the other slots stopped providing. Written against the new primitives.

    Gate · the inert count for both slots is inside the filler quota agreed in Part Four.

  7. Gloves and greaves sweep

    The reaction slot gets built rather than migrated — it starts from zero OnAdjacentActivate. Greaves finish their tempo intake. This is where catalog_shape.rs turns green.

    Gate · catalog_shape.rs green. First time all five axes are enforced.

  8. Re-pin the dependent systems

    Every rewritten piece silently rebalances a rung, because monsters wear the same catalog: all 57 boards get replayed and compared to the PR 1 baseline before any pin moves. Then rating weights, shop pools, the auto-builder's heuristic, and the progression pins — weights adjusted, thresholds never.

    Gate · every moved constant carries a one-line justification in the commit.

  9. Words, and the final measurement

    Theme entries for new and renamed pieces, naming.rs coverage for the moved mechanics, glossary and per-slot identity lines routed through theme vocabulary. Then the acceptance criteria, measured against PR 1.

    Gate · the four acceptance criteria below.

Part SixThree decisions, settled

Each of these changes what the shape test asserts. All three are now answered, and catalog_shape.rs is written against them.

Decided · exclusivity across the floating kinds

Keep identity off the floaters. Material and Plating become the designated bleed carriers — deliberately neutral, deliberately shared. Identity-carrying mechanics live only on the kinds that cannot cross a grid: Mold, Ring, Crest, Frame, Base and Layer.

This makes the exclusivity table enforceable rather than aspirational, and it stops the bleed structure from being invented — Gloves↔Greaves is the Material channel and Helmet↔Greaves is the Plating channel, both already shipped. fits() is not narrowed, so no existing board or share code is invalidated.

Decided · the filler quota

Staged: 30% for this rewrite, 15% as the written target. All five axes and the full exclusivity table ship without the 130–150 piece authoring cliff. catalog_shape.rs encodes 30% as the assertion with the 15% target recorded beside it as the documented next step, so the tightening is a change to one constant rather than a re-litigation.

Decided · underlay

Build it at PR 4, ahead of the sweeps. The catalog sweep then writes pieces against it — including the Keystone Base from the design brief — instead of approximating chest's structural tense with adjacency and revisiting later. It still merges alone.
