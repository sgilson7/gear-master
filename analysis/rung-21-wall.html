The Rung 21 Wall

A board packed as tightly as the solver can manage, from the whole catalogue, with unlimited gold, wins every rung to 20 and then stops. Rungs 29 to 50 are unreachable — and four of the eight rungs it does fail are lost to the sixty-second clock while thousands of health ahead.

1× Medium difficulty 50 rungs, each fought fresh 21 profile runs 1,473 single-item swaps 7 build profiles · 3 seeds

What the run found

five findings

CriticalThe clock is an invisible loss condition

8 / 50

Eight rungs end in a stalemate at the sixty-second cap, and a stalemate costs a run life exactly as dying does. Four of them are lost from far ahead: rung 21 with the player +7,779 health up, rung 32 at +3,336, rung 27 at +1,229, rung 39 at +979. There is nothing on screen that tells a player a clock is running, so this reads as the game taking a win away for no stated reason.

CriticalThe ladder outruns the catalogue at rung 21

r28

The deepest win any profile manages is rung 28, and no profile wins anything past rung 32. That is with perfect packing, infinite money and every component in the game available at once — conditions no real run enjoys. Monster health climbs 1,580 → 9,400 across rungs 20 to 50, a six-fold rise; gear alone has no answer that scales with it.

HighHow much you pack beats what you pack

1,383 / 1,473

Swap any single item into an otherwise fixed board at rung 20 and it wins: 322 of 322 helmets, 287 of 287 chestpieces, 252 of 252 gloves. Only the weapon slot can lose the fight, and only 35 of 348 times. The gap between the best and worst item in a slot is consistently smaller than the gain from fitting one more item — so the shop is a weak decision and the grid is the whole game.

HighTwo fountain choices are downgrades

−2,461

Measured one at a time against the same board, Bloodletter costs 2,461 health at rung 25 and Archmage costs 1,192. Juggernaut, Druid, Spellblade and Wanderer are all mildly negative through the mid game. Geomancer changes nothing at any rung tested — Resonance acts on casts, and the board the fingerprint offered it to is a martial one that never casts.

MediumDamage does not predict depth

10 dps → r32

The dense packer carries 183 dps and reaches rung 28. The per-second packer carries 87 and reaches 32. A non-assembler profile — a deliberately incompetent build with 10 dps and six items — wins 27 fights, more than the optimiser's 23. Survival and tempo decide the ladder; damage is close to noise.

The ladder, rung by rung

dense-packed board, 1×

Margin is health left on your side minus health left on theirs, at the moment the fight ends. Bars share one scale, so the lengths are comparable across the whole ladder. The first twenty rungs are never in doubt; from rung 21 the board is either killed or timed out, with three isolated wins at 23, 25 and 28 where the monster happens to be softer than its neighbours.

Three specific pieces worth a look

Kingmaker Hilt

0 / 35

Every weapon that fails at rung 20 is built on this handle — 35 losses out of 348 weapons measured, and all of them Kingmaker. It is not obviously weak: at 16 strength and 25 power it lands the biggest single hit of any handle tested. Something about the way it pays for that is costing the fight, and it is reproducible enough to bisect.

The Growing Weight

×3 stacks

The best chestpieces are simply this piece repeated: Bulwark Base + The Growing Weight ×3 scores 4,286. A component that is best used three times over is not a decision, and it prices as though it were one.

Ossuary Frame + Lonely Plating

93g

The cheapest winning helmet in the game at 93 gold, scoring 1,173 where the best helmet scores 5,416 at 295. Three times the price buys four and a half times the work, so the curve is honest here — this one is a bargain rather than a break, and useful as the reference point the others are priced against.

What to change

in order of leverage

  1. Settle the clock on health, not on nothing

    A sixty-second stalemate currently costs a run life. Award the win to whoever holds the larger share of their maximum health when the cap lands, and the four rungs lost from thousands ahead become wins. If the cap must stay punitive, put a visible timer on the screen — a hidden rule that takes a won fight away is the worst version of this.

  2. Flatten the back half of the ladder

    Health runs 1,580 at rung 20 to 9,400 at rung 50 while the best possible board is flat. Either bend that curve down, or give the player a multiplicative source of scaling in the back half — boss drops and doubled classes are the obvious levers and are already built. Rungs 29 to 50 are currently content nobody can see.

  3. Make the shop a decision again

    Every helmet, chestpiece and glove in the game wins the rung-20 fight. If item choice is meant to matter, the spread within a slot has to be wider than the value of one more item — either widen the spread or shrink the grid so packing stops being free.

  4. Never offer a class that cannot help

    Geomancer moved nothing at any rung on a martial board, and Bloodletter and Archmage were actively negative in the mid game. Gate the fountain's offers on whether the build can use the power — the fingerprint already knows enough to tell.

  5. Reprice the four components every build contains

    The Growing Weight, Gluttonous Fang, Warplate Greave and Unshod Signet appear in roughly a third of all optimal items each. That is fine if they are meant to be staples and wrong if they are meant to be choices; either way the rating model does not currently know they are special.

  6. Find out what Kingmaker Hilt is doing

    It has the highest per-hit damage of any handle and is the only handle that loses. Both cannot stay true.

How this was measured

and what it does not cover

Four ignored reports in crates/engine/tests/packing.rs, all at Medium (1×):

cargo test --release -p gearmaster-engine --test packing -- --ignored --nocapture \ how_far_each_profile_gets # 50 rungs x 7 profiles x 3 seeds, ~5 min ladder_curve # margin and outcome per rung find_outlier_gear # 1,473 single-item swaps at rung 20 what_classes_are_worth # each class alone, at five rungs

Caveats that matter. Every profile fights all fifty rungs with one fixed board and no progression — no shopping between fights, no boss drops, no fountains except in the class report, where they are held one at a time. A real run has all of those, so these numbers are a floor on what is reachable, not a ceiling. They are also all Grinder mode, and all classless except where stated.

One correction worth recording. An earlier pass of this analysis measured margin from CombatLog::player, which holds the combatant as it started — the interface lays the two boards out from it. That made margin a constant per rung that no fight could move, and produced a smooth, convincing curve along with the conclusion that all twelve classes changed nothing at any of fifty rungs. Every number above is read from the fight's own events instead, and two regression tests now guard the mistake.
